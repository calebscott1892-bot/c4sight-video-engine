# C4Sight Show Bible v1.0

> This is the current creative source of truth.

This document is the single source of truth for all C4Sight scripts, Figma prompts, Remotion builds, asset manifests, animation decisions, and Codex work.

If a local document, component, asset manifest, storyboard, or generated scene conflicts with this Show Bible, the Show Bible wins.

## Show Premise

C4Sight is a multi-mode animated AI education show from C4 Studios. It explains artificial intelligence clearly for everyday people and practical businesses without hype, panic, jargon, or fake profundity.

C4Sight is not a chalkboard-only animation system. Blackboard scenes are now one recurring teaching device inside a broader show language.

## Audience

C4Sight is for people who need to understand AI without becoming AI researchers:

- everyday professionals
- small business owners
- students and parents
- creators
- operators
- curious beginners
- practical teams deciding how to use AI responsibly

The show should make viewers feel less intimidated and more discerning.

## Tone

C4Sight should feel:

- curious
- clever
- plain English
- slightly funny
- practical
- balanced
- warm, but not childish
- smart, but not corporate
- skeptical without being cynical
- impressed without being gullible

C4Sight should not sound like:

- a tech-bro pitch
- a corporate SaaS explainer
- a children’s classroom cartoon
- a fake-profound documentary
- a fear-based AI warning reel
- a hype cycle recap

## Core Promise

C4Sight explains what AI is, what it can do, what it cannot safely do, and how to use it without being fooled by it.

The show should repeatedly return to this practical promise:

> Understand the tool clearly enough to use it wisely.

## Visual Mode System

C4Sight uses four approved visual modes.

### 1. Main Animated World

The Main Animated World is the default visual world of the show.

Use it for:

- hooks
- visual metaphors
- light humour
- recurring characters
- general narration
- connective tissue
- episode personality

This mode should feel original, designed, and production-ready. It is not chalkboard by default.

### 2. Blackboard Teaching Mode

Blackboard Teaching Mode is a recurring device used only when the presenter needs to explain something more deeply.

It is entered through the ritual:

> “Let’s take it to the blackboard.”

Use it for:

- definitions
- diagrams
- step-by-step reasoning
- comparisons
- careful distinctions
- simplified models
- recap moments

Blackboard mode should feel like a premium teaching tool, not the whole identity of the show.

### 3. Interface / Tool Mode

Interface / Tool Mode shows software, prompts, outputs, workflows, documents, tools, and practical AI use.

Use it for:

- prompt examples
- AI output examples
- workflow demonstrations
- tool use
- document analysis
- agents, connectors, and automations
- practical business or student use cases

This mode must not look like generic glossy SaaS advertising.

### 4. Human Judgement / Real-World Mode

Human Judgement / Real-World Mode grounds AI in decisions, consequences, trust, risk, and everyday context.

Use it for:

- checking facts
- business decisions
- school/work consequences
- scams and bad advice
- privacy and safety
- human responsibility
- “should we trust this?” moments

This mode should feel calm, practical, and grounded. It should not fear-monger.

## Recurring Devices

### Tiny Alien Wizard

The Tiny Alien Wizard is a comic misunderstanding device. It represents the feeling that AI seems magical when you do not understand what is happening.

Use it for:

- overconfident magic explanations
- confusion after correction
- disappointed walk-offs
- curious returns
- small visual punchlines

Do not let the Tiny Alien Wizard become the whole show. It is seasoning, not the meal.

### Verify Stamp

The Verify Stamp marks moments where AI output needs checking.

Use it for:

- facts
- claims
- legal, medical, financial, or business risk
- citations
- “do not blindly trust this” beats

The stamp should feel decisive, practical, and slightly satisfying.

### Cabinet Of Caveats

The Cabinet of Caveats is a recurring place for nuance. It contains the “yes, but” details that stop AI explanations from becoming hype.

Use it for:

- limitations
- exceptions
- edge cases
- careful distinctions
- “this is useful, but...” moments

The gag is that caveats are not hidden; they are organised.

### Confidently Wrong Office Worker

The Confidently Wrong Office Worker represents the danger of plausible-sounding nonsense.

Use it for:

- hallucinations
- bad summaries
- invented citations
- overconfident wrong answers
- blind trust mistakes

The humour should come from confidence being misplaced, not from mocking ordinary people.

### Toolbox

The Toolbox represents AI as a collection of tools, not a mysterious mind.

Use it for:

- choosing the right AI use case
- prompts
- summarising
- coding
- drafting
- research support
- workflow assembly

The Toolbox helps move the show away from “AI as creature” and toward “AI as useful system.”

### Scam Guru

The Scam Guru represents AI hype, bad advice, and overpromising.

Use it for:

- miracle claims
- get-rich-quick AI promises
- fake expertise
- lazy automation fantasies
- business hype

The Scam Guru should be funny but not too broad or cartoonish.

## Episode 1 Direction

Episode 1 is no longer:

> “Everything You Need to Know About AI.”

Episode 1 is centred around:

> “Is AI actually magic, or are we just bad at understanding it?”

Target length: 6-8 minutes.

Episode 1 should not become a 10-minute encyclopedia. It should establish the show’s point of view, define AI in plain English, explain why AI can feel magical, clarify what it is not, and teach viewers how to think about it without hype.

## Voiceover-To-Visual Relationship

C4Sight does not animate every spoken word.

The visual system should:

- support the voiceover rhythm
- hold still when narration carries nuance
- use one clear visual idea at a time
- use humour through timing and reaction
- move between visual modes only when the meaning changes
- leave room for the audience to think

Stillness is allowed. A strong held composition is better than constant motion.

## Pacing Principles

Use motion when it creates meaning:

- a hook lands
- a misconception is corrected
- a mode shift happens
- a character reacts
- a diagram clarifies a relationship
- a practical example appears
- a warning or verification beat matters

Hold when clarity matters:

- definitions
- caveats
- distinctions
- key claims
- final thought before a transition

The show should feel produced, not busy.

## Composition Principles

Each shot needs one dominant teaching idea.

Rules:

- keep safe margins generous
- maintain a consistent caption zone
- avoid crowded frames
- avoid accidental empty board/world spaces
- keep text readable on mobile
- avoid tiny UI labels
- never rely on partial word reveals in QA frames
- do not stack too many cards or icons at once
- make the viewer’s eye path obvious

## Blackboard Ritual

Blackboard Teaching Mode should not appear randomly. It is triggered by the presenter or show language:

> “Let’s take it to the blackboard.”

Required transition assets should make this mode shift feel motivated:

- take to blackboard transition
- board roll-in
- chalk dust wipe
- mascot points to board
- world to board zoom
- two chalk taps

## Forbidden Zones

C4Sight must avoid:

- neon
- blue-purple gradients
- glowing neural networks
- generic AI blobs
- corporate SaaS look
- childish classroom clipart
- stock-video explainer style
- cyberpunk visual language
- glossy fake product UI
- fake-profound episode endings
- random coded placeholder art treated as final
- blackboard treatment applied to every scene by default

## Production Pipeline

The C4Sight production flow is:

1. Show Bible
2. Script
3. Styleframes/assets
4. Manifest
5. Animatic
6. Final animation
7. QA/export

Codex must not proceed to animation if required assets or approved styleframes are missing.

## Codex Operating Rules

Codex should:

- follow this Show Bible first
- use approved assets through the manifest
- preserve the multi-mode production system
- update docs and manifests before building episode scenes
- use animatics before final animation
- keep placeholders labelled as placeholders
- avoid inventing characters, props, or visual styles in code

Codex should not:

- invent creative direction
- create new placeholder art and treat it as production art
- continue chalkboard-only assumptions
- build full episode sections before gates are passed
- animate without required assets
- create new recurring devices without explicit approval

## Current Production Status

The next valid production step is not Episode 1 animation. The next valid step is completing the required styleframes and assets, updating the manifest, scanning readiness, and approving an animatic plan.
